About

I work on automatic software optimisation: search methods that change code, models and AI agents, and keep the changes that measure better. I have worked on this since my PhD at University College London, and since 2017 as co-founder and CTO of TurinTech AI, a UCL spinout in London.

The idea has stayed the same. Generate candidate versions of a program: at first by swapping data structures and tuning parameters, now also with language models proposing edits. Check that each still passes the tests and gives the same outputs. Run it on the real workload, measure several objectives at once, keep the better ones, search again. It is the same family as genetic improvement and, more recently, evolutionary search with language models in the loop, as in FunSearch and AlphaEvolve, applied here to existing production code. TurinTech's product, Artemis, is this loop built for production use; its Discovery mode runs it on its own from a stated goal. Changes it found have been merged by the maintainers of NVIDIA TensorRT-LLM, llama.cpp and OpenAI Whisper (pull requests and benchmarks). My part: the original research and the first Artemis, in my thesis; the design of the current system; and the first version of Discovery. Since then I have been the research lead who also owns the product: I set the research questions and the experiments with the team, and they do most of the building and the writing.

The specialty is evolutionary search: genetic improvement, multi-objective optimisation, choosing what to try next. Around it I have working knowledge of machine-learning models, reinforcement learning and, since 2023, language models, because the work needed them. What I want to work on next is search that improves its own search: systems that form hypotheses about a codebase or a model, run the experiments, and use what they learn to choose the next ones. Our work on evolving LLM-based agents is a first step.

I studied at the University of Piraeus (BSc informatics, first class) and the University of Edinburgh (MSc computer science with distinction), started research at Edinburgh, and moved to UCL for the PhD. The thesis was published at ESEC/FSE, ICSE, ASE and SSBSE and received an ACM GECCO Humies award in 2019. Before TurinTech I worked in investment banking. Much of my time has gone into teaching and supervising: the Extreme Computing lab (Hadoop, Spark) at Edinburgh; MSc projects at UCL, Imperial and other universities, often placed as internships in industry; and PhD students who work on our problems, including as industrial supervisor at the University of Surrey. Most of my papers are joint work, through Knowledge Transfer Partnerships with Leeds, Warwick, Surrey and West London, a Horizon 2020 grant, and collaborators at UCL and King's College London. Away from work I play 9-a-side football.
